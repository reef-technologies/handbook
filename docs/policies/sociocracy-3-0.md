We organize the company using **Sociocracy 3.0 (S3)**, a framework for collaboration and organizational design.
S3 distributes decision making to the people closest to the work while providing clear structures for accountability, governance, and continuous improvement.

Unlike traditional hierarchical organizations, S3 emphasizes decentralized authority.
Under this operating framework, employees can, and often do, make decisions that impact how the company operates.
Decisions are made by consent, meaning a proposal is adopted unless there is a reasoned objection, as opposed to consensus, which seeks unanimous agreement and is often very difficult to achieve in practice.
This encourages decisions to be made quickly and incrementally.
Rather than seeking perfect solutions, we can adopt "good enough for now, safe enough to try" proposals, inspect the results, and adapt over time.

To streamline the decision-making progress, we have organized ourselves into multiple, functional circles that are each responsible for a clearly defined domain and are empowered to make decisions within that domain.
Circles typically operate autonomously within their respective domain, but many initiatives require cooperation across multiple circles.

## Admin Circle

The Admin Circle provides governance and coordination for the company as a whole.
It defines company-wide policies and is responsible for delegating issues that span multiple circles.
Examples of the Admin Circle’s responsibilities are the company’s overall security policy and how to improve remote team building experiences.

## Engineering Circle

The Engineering Circle owns the development, operation, and maintenance of our technical infrastructure and decides on tools and best engineering practices.
Examples of the Engineering Circle’s responsibilities are optimizing our issue tracking software and determining use cases for AI agents to help us in our work.

## Tactical Circle

The Tactical Circle is responsible for the coordination of day-to-day activities, ensuring teams can work effectively and are not blocked, and managing the company’s resources.

## Strategic Circle

The Strategic Circle focuses on the company's long term direction.
It evaluates market opportunities, defines strategic priorities, assesses major investments, and ensures that company initiatives align with our mission and long term goals.
Examples of the Strategic Circle’s responsibilities are deciding which broad direction the company should move in and which client projects should be pursued.

## Recruitment Circle

The Recruitment Circle is responsible for finding, evaluating, and hiring new employees.
It develops and maintains the entire recruitment process.

## HR Circle

At any organization, sensitive and critical HR decisions sometimes need to be made with privacy, care, and discretion.
To handle these complex matters in Reef Technologies, we have established a HR Circle - a sub-group within the wider "root" Sociocracy circle, staffed by those few of us who have the necessary level of experience to not just do the right thing, but to do it the right way.

The HR Circle is endowed with specific authorities:

- **Termination of Staff Members**:
  The HR Circle has the authority to terminate any staff member at its sole discretion, with the exception of the Sociocracy Circle members, unless special conditions apply.
- **Termination of Sociocracy Circle Members**:
  The HR Circle may also terminate a Sociocracy Circle member, but only in urgent situations, such as sabotage, theft, gross violation of the terms of legal agreement etc.
  The decision must be explained to the Sociocracy Circle afterward.

#### Notes:

- While the Sociocracy Circle does not need to be involved in the decision-making process for terminating a staff member, the HR Circle may consult with them if there is uncertainty or concern about potential reactions.
- If a Sociocracy Circle member's (non-urgent) termination is being considered, they are allowed to be present in the discussion.
